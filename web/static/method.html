Price Signal

How Price Signal works

A clear explanation of the daily price data, the checks behind each estimate, and the limits to keep in mind.

From daily prices to a careful estimate

Much of Colombo's fresh produce passes through Dambulla first. When the Dambulla wholesale price moves, Colombo prices have sometimes followed over the next few report days. Price Signal looks for that pattern and shows both a middle estimate and a possible range.

  1. Every working day the Central Bank's Daily Price Report is read, table by table, and added to the price history. A report that cannot be read is left out, never guessed.
  2. For each item and Colombo price, a statistical model compares the current Dambulla-to-Colombo difference with its usual level and with recent movements in both prices. It then estimates the change over the following 3 report days.
  3. The possible range is based on the size of errors made on past prices that were kept out of model fitting. It is deliberately wide to show uncertainty rather than hide it.
  4. A guard checks the data again on every page view. If the report is more than 3 working days old, a newer report exists that has not been read, a market was closed, a price is missing, there is too little history, or the signal is too small, the page shows the reason and no number.

Does Dambulla improve the Colombo estimate?

This check compares Price Signal with a simpler estimate that uses only Colombo's own price history. A value above zero means the Dambulla information improved accuracy on prices kept out of model fitting. The lines show the uncertainty around each result.

Does the relationship stay useful over time?

The calculation is rebuilt before each of three separate 2026 test periods. The matching Dambulla item should improve the Colombo estimate; a deliberately wrong Dambulla item should not. This helps detect a result that works only by chance.

How often did the possible range include the real change?

The target is about 8 out of 10 outcomes. The shaded band marks the acceptable result set before the check: between 75% and 85%.

How did it perform in a buying simulation?

This historical test lets a simulated buyer purchase once, either immediately or 3 report days later. A value below zero means the simulated approach cost less than always buying immediately. Perfect hindsight is shown only as an unreachable comparison.

Important limits

  • The buying comparison is a historical simulation, not a guarantee. It does not include spoilage, shortages, transport costs or the difficulty of delaying a purchase.
  • The main evaluation covers prices from 2024 onward. Separate rolling checks cover later periods in 2026, but neither check recreates a major disruption such as the 2022 crisis.
  • The relationship is modest and applies only to the 18 to 19 items that also have a Dambulla wholesale price.
  • Three buyers completed short usability sessions. Their responses helped improve the wording, but they do not prove long-term use, changed purchases or realised savings. The revised wording still needs a follow-up buyer check.

Data and supporting evidence

The prices come from the Statistics Department of the Central Bank of Sri Lanka's Daily Price Reports. The charts above are generated directly from the project's saved evaluation results: main evaluation, rolling-period checks, model checks and buying simulation. The public repository makes the calculations traceable. Price Signal is an independent student project and is not an official Central Bank service.
