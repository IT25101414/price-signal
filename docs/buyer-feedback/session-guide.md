# Buyer feedback session guide

Target duration: 12 to 15 minutes. Test one person at a time.

## Opening and consent

Say:

> I am testing the Price Signal website, not testing you. It estimates whether a Dambulla wholesale-price change may reach a selected Colombo market. It is research information, not buying advice. The session takes about 15 minutes. I will record anonymous notes and short quotations for my student project, but not your name, phone number or business address. You may skip a question or stop at any time. Is that okay?

Record `yes` before continuing. If the answer is no, thank the person and do not record the session.

## Background questions

1. What type of food business do you buy for?
2. How often do you buy vegetables?
3. Which Colombo market or supplier do you normally use?
4. What information do you currently use when deciding whether to buy today or wait?

Do not request revenue, profit or exact purchasing volumes.

## Tasks

For each task, avoid explaining the interface. Mark it `completed`, `completed with help` or `not completed`, note the approximate time, and write down any misunderstanding.

1. **Current card:** On Today, choose an item you buy and a market you know. Explain in your own words what the signal says.
2. **Uncertainty:** Point to the middle estimate and the likely range. Explain what each means and whether the wording is clear.
3. **No-signal safety:** Find or describe a `no clear signal` or `insufficient data` state. Ask what the participant thinks it means.
4. **History:** On Explore, compare Dambulla and the selected Colombo market over six months. Ask what the shared baseline date and paired report-day count mean.
5. **Weekly report:** On Weekly, explain one line and any blank value. Ask whether the report helps with planning purchases.

## Closing questions

Ask these without suggesting a preferred answer:

1. On a scale from 1 (not at all) to 5 (completely), how well do you understand the main card?
2. On a scale from 1 to 5, how much would you trust this as one input to a buying decision?
3. On a scale from 1 to 5, how useful is this for your work?
4. Would it change when you check prices, when you buy, or where you buy? Why or why not?
5. What is the most confusing part?
6. What one change would make it more useful?
7. May I use your anonymous responses and short quotations in the project report? (`yes` or `no`)

## Observer rules

- Do not call the estimate a forecast of a guaranteed price.
- Do not promise savings or recommend `buy`, `wait` or `sell`.
- Separate what the participant actually said from the observer's interpretation.
- Record critical incidents verbatim where possible, but keep quotations short and anonymous.
